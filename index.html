Under the Hood

How the math works

Four straightforward calculations built for irregular income reality.

1
Gross to Net Profit

We subtract your business expenses from your gross income to find your actual profit. This is the number your taxes are calculated on, not your revenue.

2
Tax Reserve

Your effective tax rate is applied to your average monthly profit. This monthly reserve amount goes into a separate account, untouched until tax time.

3
Average Take-Home

After taxes and your emergency buffer contribution come out of your average profit, what remains is your real take-home pay in a normal month.

4
Worst-Month Floor

We run the same math using your lowest income month to find your floor spending number. Your fixed costs need to stay under this number for your plan to be sustainable.

A common starting point is 25-30% of your net profit (income minus business expenses). The exact amount depends on your total income, province or state, and deductions. The worst-month safe spending number is what you can responsibly spend on personal living costs during your lowest-income months, after setting aside taxes and your emergency buffer contribution. It is the number your fixed costs need to stay under to keep your finances stable year-round. This worst month budget calculator surfaces that floor automatically so you can plan around it.
Business expenses are costs directly tied to running your work: software subscriptions, equipment, professional fees, marketing, a home office portion, vehicle use for work, and similar items. Personal costs like rent, groceries, and personal subscriptions are not business expenses and should be entered under personal fixed costs.
Use your effective tax rate, which is the actual percentage of your total income that goes to taxes after all brackets are applied. For most self-employed individuals in Canada and the US earning between $40,000 and $100,000 net, this falls between 18% and 32%. If unsure, 25% is a reasonable conservative starting point for your self-employed take-home calculator estimate.
A negative margin means your fixed costs exceed what you can safely spend even in a lower income month after taxes and buffers. Your options are: raise your rates to increase income, reduce fixed expenses, or reduce your emergency buffer contribution temporarily. The goal is to get your fixed costs well below your worst-month safe spending number.
