Quick Answer

How much does it cost to raise a child in 2026?

According to LendingTree's April 2026 analysis, the average American family spends $303,418 raising a child from birth to age 18. The USDA breaks this down as: housing (29%), food (18%), childcare (16%), transportation (15%), healthcare (9%), clothing (6%), and miscellaneous (7%). Costs vary by up to 60% between states. In Canada, StatsCan data shows a range of $250,000 to $370,000 CAD depending on province. These figures do not include college or the opportunity cost of a parent reducing work hours.

    LendingTree's April 2026 analysis puts the national US average at $303,418 from birth to age 18. This accounts for inflation and regional cost differences. The USDA's category breakdown allocates 29% to housing, 18% to food, 16% to childcare, 15% to transportation, 9% to healthcare, 6% to clothing, and 7% to miscellaneous expenses. In Canada, the range is $250,000 to $370,000 CAD depending on province.
    The general guideline is that child-related costs should not exceed 30-35% of your after-tax household income. On a $75,000 salary, that means roughly $1,600-$1,800 per month for child costs. Use this calculator with your exact income and location to get a personalized verdict. The biggest levers you can pull are childcare (family help vs. daycare saves $10,000-$20,000/year) and education (public vs. private).
    Massachusetts, New York, and California consistently rank as the most expensive states, with total costs exceeding $350,000-$400,000 from birth to 18. Hawaii and Connecticut also rank near the top. The least expensive states include Mississippi, Arkansas, and West Virginia, where total costs can be under $230,000. The main drivers of state-level differences are housing costs, daycare rates, and healthcare premiums.

    The Canada Child Benefit (CCB) provides up to $7,787 per year per child under 6, and $6,570 per year per child aged 6-17 (2025/2026 rates). For a family earning $75,000 CAD, the CCB can offset $80,000-$100,000 of total child-rearing costs over 18 years. Quebec families benefit further from $10/day daycare. When you factor in CCB, the net cost of raising a child in Canada drops substantially, particularly for lower and middle-income families.
