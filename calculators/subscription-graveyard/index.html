Quick Audit

Find every subscription you are paying for

Run through this in 30 minutes. Most people recover $50-$100/month.

  • Search your email inbox for "receipt", "invoice", "payment confirmation", and "your subscription" -- set the date range to the last 24 months.
  • Review 3 months of bank and credit card statements line by line. Flag every recurring charge, including small ones under $5.
  • Check your App Store or Google Play subscriptions directly. On iPhone: Settings > your name > Subscriptions. On Android: Play Store > Payments & subscriptions.
  • Review apps connected via "Sign in with Google" or "Sign in with Apple" -- many have paid tiers. Google: myaccount.google.com/permissions. Apple: Settings > Apple ID > Password & Security > Sign in with Apple.
A 30-minute subscription audit saves the average person $50-$100/month. That is $600 to $1,200 per year recovered from charges you were barely aware of. The hardest part is starting.
Methodology

How the calculation works

Three pieces of industry data drive the numbers. No black box.

1
Category averages set the baseline
Each category uses real market pricing. Streaming averages $14/month, AI tools $20, fitness $15, software $12, subscription boxes $35, memberships $45. The total gives you a credible monthly baseline without requiring you to remember every charge.
2
42% zombie rate applied to find waste
Research across multiple consumer spending studies consistently finds that roughly 42% of active subscriptions are either rarely used or completely forgotten. Your monthly waste estimate applies that rate to your total, then multiplies by 12 for the annual figure.
3
The perception gap reveals the real number
The average American thinks they spend $111/month on subscriptions but actually spends $273/month. That 62% underestimate is your blind spot. The "what you think you spend" figure uses that gap: your estimated total multiplied by 0.41. The actual spend is the full calculated total from the subscription audit tool.
4
The uncertain toggle adds a buffer
Toggling on "any you are not sure about" adds 15% to your total before all calculations run. This accounts for subscriptions you cannot immediately name but are likely still paying for. Most people find 1-3 they forgot about during a bank statement audit.

Common Questions

Frequently asked questions

The average American spends $273/month on subscriptions but thinks they spend only $111/month. That is a 62% underestimate -- a blind spot that adds up to nearly $2,000 per year in charges people have mentally written off. This subscription spending calculator uses industry averages per category to surface a realistic total based on how many services you actually have.
Zombie subscriptions are active paid subscriptions you no longer actively use but are still being charged for. Industry research suggests roughly 42% of all subscriptions fall into this category. They survive because companies rely on friction and forgetfulness to keep them billed. A subscription audit every 6-12 months is the most reliable way to kill them.
The goal of this calculator is to show you how much you are probably spending even without remembering every subscription. Most people cannot list their subscriptions accurately from memory, which is exactly why subscription creep happens. The category averages are based on real pricing data and give you a credible baseline. If you know your exact costs, your actual total is likely close to or higher than what this calculator shows.
The most reliable approach is to search your email inbox for keywords like "receipt", "invoice", "payment confirmation", and "subscription". Then review 2-3 months of credit card and bank statements line by line. Pay close attention to amounts you do not immediately recognize. Most people find 3-5 forgotten subscriptions this way within 30 minutes.
Subscription creep is the gradual accumulation of recurring charges that individually feel small but collectively drain hundreds of dollars each month. It happens because sign-up is easy and cancellation is buried. To stop it: do a quarterly subscription audit, use a dedicated card for subscriptions so charges are easy to spot, and treat any new subscription as a commitment to cancel something else first.
