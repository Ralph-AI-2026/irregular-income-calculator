Under the Hood

How Tariffs Affect Your Shopping Cart

Tariffs are taxes paid at the border. Retailers pass most of that cost to consumers. Here is how it flows through to your household budget.

Tariff Levied at the Border

When imported goods enter the country, an importer pays a percentage of the declared value as a tariff. In 2026, rates vary from 4.5% on food to 33% on clothing depending on category and country of origin.

Cost Passed to Retailers

Importers and wholesalers pass most of the tariff cost downstream to retailers. Research consistently shows that 80-100% of tariff costs eventually reach the shelf price, with consumers absorbing the majority.

Higher Prices on the Shelf

You pay more at checkout even if the product looks identical to last year. Clothing and electronics are most visibly affected because nearly all of these goods are manufactured overseas and fully subject to import tariffs.

Ongoing Annual Drain

Unlike a one-time purchase, tariff-inflated prices compound every month. A household spending $250/month on clothing faces $99 in tariff costs annually on that category alone before the year ends.

Common Questions

Tariff Calculator FAQ

Grocery tariff costs are estimated at a 4.5% surcharge on your monthly grocery spend. This reflects the blended average tariff burden on imported food products including produce, packaged goods, and processed foods affected by current 2026 trade policy. Not all groceries carry the same rate, but 4.5% is a reasonable household average based on the typical import mix in a North American grocery basket.
Clothing and footwear are among the most heavily tariffed import categories. The majority of apparel sold in North America is manufactured overseas, and 2026 tariff schedules have pushed effective rates on these goods significantly higher. A 33% surcharge estimate reflects the blended impact across fast fashion, mid-market, and branded footwear where the import-affected portion is very high.
The 25% car tariff primarily applies to new imported vehicles and affects the import-content portion of domestically assembled vehicles. This calculator estimates that 40% of a car's price is import-affected, which is a reasonable middle estimate across both fully imported models and domestically assembled vehicles with significant imported parts content. Used cars may see price pressure indirectly as new car supply tightens and costs rise.
The rates used in this calculator are estimates based on publicly reported 2026 tariff schedules and economic analyses. Actual consumer impact varies by retailer, supply chain structure, product origin, and whether costs have been fully passed through. This tool is designed to give households a reasonable planning estimate, not a precise legal figure. Always check current government tariff schedules for exact rates on specific products.
Several practical steps can reduce your exposure: buy domestically produced goods where available (especially for clothing and produce), delay large electronics purchases if possible, shop secondhand for clothing and appliances, consider delaying a vehicle purchase or buying used, and consolidate grocery trips to avoid impulse imports. Knowing your tariff cost number is the first step to making intentional substitution decisions.
